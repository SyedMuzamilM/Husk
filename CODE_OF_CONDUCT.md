# Code of conduct

Husk welcomes contributors and users of all backgrounds and experience levels. Everyone participating in its issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and project events is expected to follow this policy. It also applies when someone represents Husk in public.

## Expected conduct

- Treat people with respect, including when you disagree about a technical decision.
- Give actionable feedback about the code or behavior. Explain your reasoning and listen to corrections.
- Welcome questions and help newcomers find the information they need.
- Respect people's names, pronouns, boundaries, time, and privacy.
- Use disposable fixtures in bug reports. Redact usernames, private paths, credentials, and personal files.

Harassment, discrimination, sexualized conduct, threats, stalking, personal attacks, repeated unwanted contact, and publishing private information without consent are unacceptable. Deliberate disruption, retaliation against a reporter, and evading a moderation decision are also unacceptable.

## Reporting

Send private conduct reports to [muzamil@mzml.dev](mailto:muzamil@mzml.dev), with “Husk conduct report” in the subject. Include the relevant links or messages, what happened, and any immediate concerns. Share only the personal information needed to investigate. You can report behavior you witnessed or experienced. Security vulnerabilities follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

Reports go to the current maintainer, Syed Muzamil, [@SyedMuzamilM](https://github.com/SyedMuzamilM). Husk currently has one maintainer and cannot offer an independent internal review of a complaint about that maintainer. GitHub's own [abuse reporting process](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam) remains available for conduct on its platform.

The maintainer will acknowledge reports as availability permits, review the evidence, and share an outcome with affected people where appropriate. Reporter identities and private details will be shared only when needed to investigate, address an immediate risk, or meet a legal obligation. This is a volunteer project without a guaranteed response time.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may edit or remove content and restrict participation. Responses depend on the behavior, its impact, and whether it has happened before. They may include a request to correct behavior, a private warning, temporary restrictions, or a permanent ban. Serious threats or harassment can result in an immediate ban.

A moderation message should explain the rule, the required change, and the duration of any restriction, unless doing so would expose private information or create a safety risk. Participants may appeal by replying to the private moderation message with relevant new information. Retaliation or repeated public arguments about a private report will not substitute for that process.
